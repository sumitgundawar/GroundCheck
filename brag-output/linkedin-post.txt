Most AI demos show you what the model answered. I built one that shows you what it refused.

GroundCheck answers clinical questions using only documents you trust, and puts a citation on every sentence. When it can't back something up, it says so and sends the question to a person instead.

The refusal is the point. Ask about a medicine that isn't in its sources and it stops before any model writes a word — eleven checks run, one fails, the rest never happen — and all of it goes into an audit record you can verify.

A system that confidently invents a dose is worse than one that says "I don't know."

I tested it the way I'd want a hospital to test it: 265,778 generated questions and patient scenarios, of which 222,210 had to be refused — and all 222,210 were. Then 12,056 of the same questions asked the way people actually talk, with none wrongly refused.

Being straight about what it isn't: the demo data is synthetic on purpose, every medicine in it invented. It's not a medical device and hasn't been clinically validated. MIT licensed, runs on your own machine.
